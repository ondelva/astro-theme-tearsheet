Every few months a port publishes a record throughput figure and a trade journal calls it a vote of confidence in the region. Sometimes it is. More often I want to know how much of the figure is transshipment, because a transshipped box is counted more than once, and the number then says less about local trade than it appears to.

A container arriving at a hub on a large ship, waiting two days and leaving on a smaller ship is lifted off the first vessel and onto the second. Most ports report this as two moves, or two TEU for a 40-foot box.

<!-- cut -->

## A worked example

Take a 40-foot box from Qingdao to Gdansk, routed via Rotterdam. At Qingdao it is loaded: one move. At Rotterdam it is discharged and later loaded onto a feeder: two moves. At Gdansk it is discharged: one move. Across the three ports that is four moves for one journey, and a statistic that counts 8 TEU for a box that is 2 TEU.[^1]

Now compare a box from Qingdao to Rotterdam with its own consignee. Loaded and discharged: two moves. The first box generates twice the throughput and creates no more economic activity at Rotterdam than the second.

## How to read a throughput figure

I ask three questions. What share of the volume is transshipment? Has it changed? And does the port count in TEU or in boxes?

Ports with a high transshipment share, such as Algeciras or Singapore, can show large swings when one carrier moves a service between hubs, with no change in local demand. Ports with a low share, such as Felixstowe, are closer to a measure of what the hinterland consumes.

Last year one northern European hub reported growth of 4.1% in total throughput. Its own breakdown, published two pages later, showed transshipment up 9.6% and import and export combined up 1.2%. The headline was true. The second figure was the one a local haulier needed.

## What changes in practice

If you plan trucking, warehouse space or chassis supply near a port, use the gateway figure and ignore the total. If you compare two ports, compare the same category in both. If a port will not split its figure, I take that as an answer.

Where the split exists, it is often a table in the annual report, six to ten pages in, headed "modal split" or "box types".

[^1]: Some ports count only the discharge, or count transshipment once. The reporting rule is usually stated in a note to the annual statistics. Where it is not stated, I assume two moves per transshipped box.
