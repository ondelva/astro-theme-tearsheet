A blank sailing is announced, in my experience, in a fairly regular order, and the order says more than the count does. Over the last ten weeks I logged 63 cancelled departures on Asia to North Europe services. For each one I recorded when it first appeared, in which document, and who put it there.

<!-- cut -->

## The usual order

The earliest sign is a quiet change in the schedule file: a departure date vanishes, or a vessel name is replaced by "TBN", to be nominated. This happened a median of 19 days before the planned sailing. Only 9 of the 63 were mentioned in any public statement at that point.

The second sign is a customer advisory sent to booked shippers, often under the phrase "network adjustment". The median gap between the schedule change and the advisory was 7 days, which leaves roughly 12 days before sailing. The third is a note on the rate sheet, usually a week later still, when the carrier starts quoting the next departure at a higher level.

So a shipper who reads advisories learns about a blank twelve days out. A shipper who reads the schedule file learns about it nineteen days out. That is a week of extra time to rebook, which on a busy lane is the difference between a slot and a rollover.

## Who goes first

Alliance members behave differently from carriers running alone. Where two members share a service, the partner's matching sailing was cancelled within 3 days in 27 of the 31 pairs I could match. A vessel-sharing agreement leaves little room for one party to blank and the other to sail. A large Asian carrier operating a standalone service announced a median of 5 days later than the alliance pairs, perhaps because it needs no one's agreement.

## What I do with it

I count the TBN entries on each trade lane every Monday. The rule I use is crude: four or more new TBN entries on one lane in one week. It preceded a rise in the spot index within three weeks in 5 of the 7 times it occurred. Seven is a small sample, and I would not stake a contract on it. I would use it to decide whether to book early.
