This is the seventh issue of Harbor Notes, and the last of the year.

The most useful thing to report is the habits that held up. The least comfortable is a forecast I did not get right.

<!-- cut -->

## What worked

Reading the schedule file on Monday was the best habit. Of the 63 blank sailings I logged, the earliest signal came from the file, and the average lead over the customer advisory was a week. I intend to keep doing it and to publish the weekly counts in January.

Keeping my own quote table beside a published index was second. The two-day lag was visible after the third week, and several readers have since written to say they found the same on other lanes. One found a lag of four days on a transpacific index, which I have not yet been able to explain.

Third was asking carriers a short, specific question and printing the answer, or the silence. Of the six questions I put to carriers this autumn, four were answered in under a week. The other two are still open. I will list them in the first January issue.

## What I got wrong

In the second issue I wrote that four or more new TBN entries on a lane in a week had preceded a spot rise in 5 of 7 cases. In the five weeks since, it has fired twice. One was followed by a rise and one was not. That makes 6 of 9, which is still more than a coin toss and far less than I implied.

I have moved the signal into a column marked "weak" and I will keep updating the count until it is either useful or dead.

## What comes next

Two things are on my list for the first quarter. One is annual contract renewals, which start in January and where the most money moves. The other is a proper look at Red Sea routing and how much of the current capacity is a detour in disguise.

Thank you for reading, and for the corrections, which have been more numerous than the praise and more valuable. Issue eight arrives on 6 January.
