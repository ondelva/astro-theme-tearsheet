## The argument comes first

The second paragraph of an issue says what I think happened. If you read nothing else, read that. The rest of the issue is there so you can disagree with it on the evidence and not on my tone.

### Sources, in order of trust

1. A carrier's booking system, read on the day.
2. Its published schedule feed.
3. A tariff PDF, which is usually right and occasionally months out of date.[^1]
4. Anything said on an earnings call.

When I quote a figure, I say which of these it came from. When two of them disagree, I print both.

## Tables are the charts

I don't draw charts. A table of lanes shows the shape of the market and still lets you copy the number.

| Lane                    | All-in spot, 40ft | Change |
| ----------------------- | ----------------: | -----: |
| Shanghai to Rotterdam   |            $3,610 |   +$40 |
| Busan to Rotterdam      |            $3,450 |   +$25 |
| Port Klang to Jebel Ali |            $1,090 |   -$15 |

The sources, by how far they run ahead of the weekly index:

| Source            | Updated    | Lag to the index |
| ----------------- | ---------- | ---------------: |
| Booking portal    | Continuous |           0 days |
| Schedule feed     | Daily      |            1 day |
| Weekly spot index | Friday     |           4 days |

> **On method.** A "loop" is one weekly service, counted once however many carriers sell space on it.

## What readers send

> I booked Vietnam cargo on a Monday after your note on refresh days, and the quote held. Keep the boring tables.
>
> A forwarder in Hamburg

Letters like that one shape what comes next. Short ones, mostly:

- a screenshot of a schedule that changed overnight,
- an invoice line that does not match the tariff,
- a question I can't answer yet.

> **Caution.** Nothing here is advice on a specific contract. Check the tariff that applies to you.

## What members get after the rule

The members part is where the evidence lives: the full service-by-service table, the tariff lines I compared and the three scenarios I would plan for. It is written for people who have to defend a number in a meeting on Wednesday.
